# Societal Impact Reformulation

This file contains the full proposed reformulation for the societal impact section.

> This work focuses on improving signal detection in time-frequency representations using deep learning methods. While motivated by radio-frequency (RF) signal analysis, the proposed approach is applicable to a broad range of domains involving time-frequency data, including acoustic monitoring (e.g., marine mammal detection), environmental sensing, seismology, and other scientific or industrial settings where weak structured signals must be identified under noisy conditions.
> 
> The potential positive impacts of this work include improved monitoring of natural environments, enhanced detection of rare or weak signals, and support for scientific research in domains where signal analysis is critical. For instance, in acoustics, such methods may contribute to biodiversity monitoring and conservation efforts. More broadly, robust time-frequency detection can support non-invasive sensing pipelines in settings where classical handcrafted approaches are brittle under distribution shifts or low-SNR conditions.
> 
> However, we acknowledge that signal detection technologies may also raise dual-use concerns. In particular, RF signal detection methods can be applied in surveillance, electronic warfare, or intelligence contexts. As such, there is a risk that these technologies could be used in ways that raise privacy, security, or ethical concerns. Similar concerns may also arise in civilian contexts if signal monitoring is deployed without appropriate safeguards, transparency, or legal oversight.
> 
> We emphasize that this work is methodological in nature and does not target any specific application domain. We encourage responsible use of such technologies and adherence to applicable legal and ethical standards.
