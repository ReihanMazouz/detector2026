We understand that the reviewer’s concern stems from the choice of multiple-resolution spectrograms over wavelets, our interpretation is expressed below:

**Does the built-in multi-resolution nature of wavelets eliminate the need for multiple complementary representations, in particular multiple STFT resolutions?**  
No. Wavelets redistribute resolution within one representation, still under the Heisenberg uncertainty principle, [A Wavelet Tour of Signal Processing,  chapter 2, section 3.2, pp. 30-32]. They do not provide multiple resolutions of the same time-frequency location, which is precisely what multiple STFT resolutions provide.

**Is the proposed methodology limited to STFT representations, or could it also be applied to multiple time-frequency representations, in particular multiple wavelet-based ones?**  
Yes. It can in principle be applied to any family of time-frequency representations, including multiple wavelet-based ones.[^1]

**In the application settings considered in this paper, is the wavelet transform better suited than the STFT or than a set of STFTs with different resolutions?**  
Not clearly. Wavelet decomposition can be well suited when the task benefits from high temporal resolution at high frequencies and high frequency resolution at low frequencies. However, in the application datasets considered in this study, relevant structures may be distributed across both time and frequency without following a strictly scale-dependent organization. In such cases, the logarithmic tiling induced by wavelets is not necessarily aligned with the underlying signal structure.

In addition, the effectiveness of wavelet-based representations depends on the choice of the mother wavelet, which implicitly encodes assumptions about the signal morphology. When little or no prior information is available about the signal class, this choice may significantly impact performance. Moreover, if one aims to analyze several signal classes with different characteristics, multiple mother wavelets may be needed. This in turn amounts to producing several representations of the same acquisition, which brings us back to the same multi-representation challenge addressed in our paper.

<span style="color:orange">By contrast, Fourier-based representations provide a generic and often sparse description for a broad class of modulated signals, which can enhance signal-to-noise contrast under standard noise models. From a detection-theoretic perspective, this relates to classical energy-based tests whose performance primarily depends on the signal-to-noise ratio (ce n'est toutefois pas toujours vrai ; par exemple, pour des signaux NLFM, on ne gagne pas nécessairement en SNR avec une STFT par rapport à une transformée en ondelette bien calibrée).</span>

Finally, scalogram representations may be less straightforward to interpret in terms of time–frequency characteristics because of the underlying notion of scale change, and may also involve longer processing times. In this context, we did not identify a clear advantage of wavelet decompositions over STFTs, which motivated the use of multiple STFT representations.

**Concluding remark.**  
We believe that the main source of confusion is that the term *multi-resolution* is used here in a different sense than in wavelet analysis. For wavelets, it refers to a single representation with location-dependent resolution. In our paper, it refers to using several complementary representations of the same signal so that the same local pattern can be observed under different resolutions.

[^1]: We remain cautious on this point because, with well-calibrated STFTs, the same signal tends to appear at the same spatial location across branches once they reach the fusion stage. This is particularly consistent with our fusion design, which combines channel concatenation, attention mechanisms including spatial attention, and subsequent filtering. This exact alignment is not necessarily preserved with other families of transforms. That said, based on our expertise, we cautiously believe that the model can still learn such scale differences and need not be fundamentally limited by this challenge. <span style="color:orange">An ablation study on this point is currently in progress and will be shared as soon as it is available: [w2_ablation_figures.md](./w2_ablation_figures.md).</span>
